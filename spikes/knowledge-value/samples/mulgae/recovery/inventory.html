Recover roles from a proven source

After Mulgae reports an incomplete or unpublished review, which exact recovery operation is admitted, which accepted roles remain usable, and what evidence makes retention usable rather than diagnostic-only?

Inspect the exact run first. For an unpublished failure with available failed-run recovery, replay each returned retry attempt with --replay exact. Accepted role results remain retained. Preparation or diagnostics alone do not establish a replay source.

M02 · Qualified inference

Treat the status list as an inventory, not a compose verdict

The status inventory alone is insufficient to establish that a role satisfies compose's accepted-role and report-integrity checks.

Snapshot.Status() places entries with outcome failed in retry_attempts and every other role entry in accepted_roles. That is the literal classification in the supplied function. M-S01 · status.go:30–35

Compose uses a narrower predicate: outcome completed or degraded, an attempt binding, and a nonblank provider instance. Its report validator checks additional identity and integrity fields. Inferring final compose admissibility from the status array would skip those checks. This distinction does not establish which additional outcomes the unseen recovery-document validator permits. M-S08 · service.go:520–528, 540–542

M03 · Direct source

Accepted recovery results are retained, not replacement targets

Recovery-source rerun rejects an attempt whose state is succeeded, protecting the accepted role from replacement through that path.

The source loader matches the requested attempt ID and returns recovery rerun cannot replace an accepted role for AttemptSucceeded. The README separately states that accepted roles are retained. M-S02 · recovery_source.go:27–33 M-S09 · README.md:761–762

Retained usefulness is conditional: the ADR requires proof of inputs, output, and cleanup. Recovery availability does not itself provide final-review findings, report, or export access; those still require a committed final review. See preservation during compose. M-S10 · ADR:7–10, 63–68

M04 · Direct source

A retained source needs evidence and successful cleanup

Preparation alone grants no replay authority; retention requires provider termination, target revalidation, and workspace release before publication installs the recovery manifest last.

The immutable source binds the captured target and archive, selected roles' initial prompts, role/provider assignments, threshold, attempts, accepted reports, findings, and verified evidence. Runtime diagnostics alone are not this proof. Security, configuration, artifact, missing-input, cleanup, and persistence failures deny recovery. M-S10 · ADR:7–28

The supplied retention implementation constructs workspace completion evidence, releases the workspace, rejects a mismatched cleanup receipt, and later calls PersistFailedRunRecovery. These are source observations, not evidence that any particular run completed them. Successful retention also leaves the original failed execution authoritative; it grants neither a successful review nor CI pass. M-S03 · failed_run_recovery.go:50–63, 68–74 M-S10 · ADR:23–28

If execution reaches the receipt comparison after workspace release returns without error, a mismatch returns recovery unavailable: recovery_cleanup_receipt_invalid before persistence. This retention refusal precedes any replay attempt selection through the source it would have installed. A matching receipt only passes that comparison: later diagnostics, context, and persistence can still fail. Earlier failures may prevent reaching the comparison, and independently failing mandatory cleanup can affect the final failure class and exit; the illustration does not predict one overall exit. M-S03 · failed_run_recovery.go:50–74 M-S10 · ADR:20–28

M05 · Direct source

The retention budget is cooperative

Retention shares one cooperative ten-minute budget with caller cancellation detached, but the ADR does not promise an overall return within ten minutes.

The deadline is not refreshed per stage. Preparation, sealing, diagnostics, provider drain, workspace release, and persistence share the remaining budget. Mandatory cleanup after retention failure or timeout may extend the overall return; an arbitrary blocking kernel filesystem call is not interrupted by this cooperative budget. Retention is synchronous, with no abandoned background writer or operator timeout knob. M-S10 · ADR:30–40

M06 · Direct source

Missing retention cannot be reconstructed from diagnostics

A missing recovery source requires a new review when authorized; historical diagnostic-only runs cannot be recovered retrospectively.

The ADR distinguishes a missing manifest, which is unavailable, from a present but invalid source, which fails closed. It excludes promotion of logs into accepted results and recovery after forced process termination or power loss. Operators must reconcile exact IDs before any retry. This explanation neither authorizes nor starts a new review. M-S09 · README.md:761–762 M-S10 · ADR:74–77

For the effect on a root or a required lineage source, continue to missing sources during compose.

## Decision

Mulgae retains failed ordinary reviews and failed reruns in a separate,
immutable `mulgae-run-recovery.v1` source. It freezes every selected role's
initial prompt before dispatch, including roles cancelled while queued. The
source binds the captured target and archive, role/provider assignments,
threshold, attempts, accepted reports, findings, and verified evidence.

Preparation grants no replay authority. Provider termination, target
revalidation, and workspace release must succeed before publication installs
the recovery manifest last. Security, configuration, artifact, missing-input,
cleanup, and persistence failures deny recovery. A retention or persistence
refusal preserves the original execution failure and exit; it cannot turn the
failed run into a successful or publishable review. If mandatory provider drain,
abort, or workspace cleanup independently fails, that failure remains in the
normal operational precedence and may determine the final failure class and
exit. Recovery never grants final-review publication or CI pass.

Lines 30–40

Failed-run retention uses one cooperative ten-minute budget created before
preparation. Caller cancellation is detached so a cancelled review can still be
retained, but the deadline is not refreshed per stage. Preparation, sealing,
retention diagnostics, provider drain, workspace release, and persistence share
that remaining budget. Retention drain keeps the shared context and clips each
attempt to one minute. Mandatory cleanup after retention failure or timeout
still uses the ordinary detached one-minute drain policy and may make the
overall return exceed ten minutes. The budget is cooperative; it does not
interrupt an arbitrary blocking kernel filesystem call. Retention stays
synchronous: there is no abandoned background writer and no operator timeout
knob.

Lines 42–46

`rerun --run ... --attempt ...` accepts either a committed review or an exact
failed-run recovery source. It rejects attempts to replace an accepted role in
a recovery source. A failed rerun may retain another recovery source under the
same rules, with its exact parent and source attempt. No provider substitution
or unconditional internal-error retry is introduced.
